# RothCpy-AFM
## A Python version of the RothC model accounting for different C-input sources in agricultural and agroforestry systems across diverse pedoclimatic conditions

Soil organic carbon (SOC) dynamics play a pivotal role in preserving soil health, supporting ecosystem services and agricultural stability, and driving climate change mitigation strategies. The *RothCpy-AFM* (Rothamsted Carbon Python - AgroForestry Model) is an open-source, scriptable Python implementation of the Rothamsted Carbon Model framework. This version of the model is designed to simulate monthly SOC turnover across agroecosystems, with specific enhancements for agroforestry, complex crop rotations, multi-source organic residue management, and diverse pedoclimatic conditions.
A core feature of *RothCpy-AFM* is its dual methodological engine, which grants users the flexibility to select between two distinct computational versions of the Rothamsted Carbon Model depending on site characteristics:  i) standard RothC (Coleman and Jenkinson, 1996): ideal for temperate and non-water-limited topsoils; and ii) RothC10N (Farina et al., 2013): by adopting a physically-based soil moisture deficit (SMD) assessment using hydraulic constants calculated via van Genuchten parameters and HYPRES pedotransfer functions, substantially improving model accuracy to simulate dryland, semi-arid, and water-stressed environments. 
While standard implementations of RothC offer robust simulations for arable croplands, they often lack the granularity required to process heterogeneous vegetation streams. RothCpy-Agro addresses these operational constraints through several key structural updates: 
multi-source organic input streams: the model explicitly decouples carbon inputs into herbaceous and tree residues, and organic amendments. By applying independent decomposable-to-resistant plant material (DPM/RPM) ratios, it accounts for differences in biochemical quality and lignin content, making it particularly effective for agroforestry and other mixed cultivation mixed systems;
flexible evapotranspiration options: the model can handle either potential evapotranspiration or open-pan evaporation values through a built-in conditional switch;
automated spin-up & forward simulation: baseline pool initialization (DPM, RPM, BIO, HUM, IOM) is achieved through an automated iterative spin-up routine executed strictly via the standard RothC algorithm. By continuously adjusting plant carbon inputs until the simulated SOC converges with the observed baseline stock at time zero, the model establishes steady-state initial conditions before launching forward scenarios driven by real historical or projected input data (climate, farm management and soil properties).
Results are automatically generated as structured dataset files (.CSV and .TXT) tracking monthly and annual carbon fluxes across all pools and the total soil organic carbon (SOC) stock, accompanied by diagnostic graphical plots. 
Designed for academic, educational, and research applications, *RothCpy-AFM* provides a scalable, batch-processable environment to quantify the long-term impacts of agricultural management, land-use change, and climate scenarios on soil carbon sequestration.
